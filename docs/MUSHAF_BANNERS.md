# Open chapter banners

Claude Opus 5.5 researched these three treatments for the developer selector.
The designs adapt manuscript heading ideas to the existing chapter ornament
grammar; they are not facsimiles of a particular manuscript.

The enclosing capsule made tall letters and long tails compete with its
horizontal rules. Centering each name's complete ink bounds then raised the
body of names with deep descenders. All three treatments instead leave the
central paper open above and below the name and use one writing line per face
and size. Arabic centres the alef-to-baseline zone; Latin centres its capital
height. A shared tail allowance protects the neighbouring text, and glyphs
retain visible overflow.

| Developer option | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Cartouche panel | Doubled ruled wings and a chapter weave flank a pointed paper window. The top and bottom rules stop at the window; its width grows with the title. Default. |
| Cloud collar | Unruled tooled ground surrounds a full-height paper opening with three gold lobes on each side. No stroke lies above or below the title. |
| Open heading | Gold lettering stands on open paper. Tapered hairlines extend its baseline toward drawn end rosettes, with small ground strips beneath the rosettes. Lines shorten or disappear for long names. |

The slot, type scale, horizontal centring, chapter seed, basmalah and body
geometry are shared. The setting persists and applies to Arabic and English.
Canvas paths are cached by size and title width; the implementation reuses
the existing weave and rosette without additional fonts, images or layers.

The research consulted heading and illumination records including
[Walters manuscript W.555](https://art.thewalters.org/files/pdf/W555.pdf),
[the Met's Mamluk folio](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/452704),
[an Ottoman section with two headings](https://www.bonhams.com/auction/22816/lot/5/a-quran-section-including-two-illuminated-headings-of-sura-xlii-al-shura-consultation-also-known-as-ain-sin-qaf-and-sura-xliv-al-dukkan-smoke-or-mist-probably-ottoman-turkey-15th-century),
and [an Ilkhanid sura heading](https://www.bonhams.com/auction/29724/lot/2/an-illuminated-sura-heading-and-a-single-line-from-a-large-ilkhanid-manuscript-of-the-quran-iraq-early-14th-century).
The Met record was available through indexed catalogue text when direct
fetches failed during the research.
