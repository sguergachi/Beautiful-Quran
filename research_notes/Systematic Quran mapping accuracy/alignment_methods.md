# Arabic–English alignment methods and existing Quran alignment resources

Research date: 13 October 2026. Scope: primary-source research and inspection of the present mapper; no inference runs, model downloads, paid calls, production changes, or accuracy certification. No private runtime text or word-card corpus is reproduced here. Conclusions below concern the frozen English edition and app word segmentation, rather than every possible translation.

## Does an authoritative complete alignment of English verse prose to Arabic spans already exist?

### Takeaway

No complete, authoritative Sahih International prose-to-Arabic span map was located in the reviewed official resources. The documented Quran Foundation API, Quranic Arabic Corpus, quran.ai tools, and a recent multimodal Quran dataset provide useful Arabic-word evidence and verse translations, but those are different resources from an alignment of every printed English token to its Arabic meaning span.

### Cited Findings

- **Quran Foundation separates word glosses from verse prose.** Its current verse endpoint uses `words=true` and `language` for word-by-word translation. A word has `position`, Arabic text fields, and `translation: {text, language_name}`. The independent verse `translations[]` records contain edition/resource identity and full translation text, potentially with HTML and footnotes. The documented word and translation schemas have no English-prose character offsets, token IDs, or Arabic-span link fields. This is a statement about the inspected published schema, not proof that QF has no unpublished alignment work. — [Official verse API schema](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/content_apis_versioned/4.0.0/verses-by-verse-key/)
- **QAC is authoritative linguistic evidence, not the missing prose alignment.** QAC documents manually annotated morphology, a syntactic treebank, and a semantic ontology. Its Arabic is Classical, fully vowelized Arabic with traditional iʿrāb analysis; the documentation explicitly distinguishes this from modern newswire treebanks. Its English page presents seven whole-verse translations separately from the Arabic morphology view. Neither inspected page advertises token/span alignments into an individual full-verse English edition. — [QAC documentation](https://corpus.quran.com/documentation/), [QAC English translation interface](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp)
- **quran.ai is a grounding service.** The official guide documents `fetch_quran`, `fetch_translation`, `fetch_tafsir`, and word morphology/paradigm/concordance tools. Morphology returns roots, lemmas, stems, grammar, morpheme segments and frequency evidence. Search/fetch pagination must be followed; translation is distinct from tafsir. These provide evidence for a reviewer’s adjudication rather than a documented prose-alignment endpoint. — [Official quran.ai MCP guide](https://github.com/quran/mcp.quran.ai/blob/master/skills/quran.ai-MCP-usage/SKILL.md)
- **Live verification:** after `fetch_grounding_rules`, I inspected the live public MCP `tools/list` on 13 October 2026 through the existing UUID-request client. No alignment tool appears in the returned tool names. `fetch_translation` accepts ayahs/editions/continuation/grounding nonce; `fetch_word_morphology` accepts an ayah and word position/text, and does not accept the nonce. These live schema observations agree with the guide. No passage interpretations are made in these notes. — [Public MCP endpoint](https://mcp.quran.ai), [Official tool usage guide](https://github.com/quran/mcp.quran.ai/blob/master/skills/quran.ai-MCP-usage/SKILL.md)
- **“Aligned words” in a new dataset still means interlinear words/audio.** Quran-MD (January 2026 preprint) describes verse records with full translations and word records with Arabic, word translation, transliteration and aligned word audio. Its construction merges quranwbw word records, verse audio and word audio. The described JSON does not provide Sahih-prose offsets linked to Arabic spans. This is a useful modern counterexample to conflating a dataset’s “word-level alignment” with this app’s task. — [Quran-MD paper, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17880v1#S3)
- **There is a genuine general Arabic–English gold benchmark.** XL-WA includes 90 English–Arabic development sentences and 210 test sentences, with 1,591 and 3,597 links respectively. Evaluation annotations were made by professional native-language annotators. Its repository says Arabic/Korean/Swedish/Chinese data access requires contacting Babelscape, and its license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. It is not a Quran/Sahih gold reference. — [XL-WA official repository](https://github.com/SapienzaNLP/XL-WA), [XL-WA paper](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/102935e2-dea3-4598-9f63-fdb5cec8061a/Martelli_XL-WA_2023.pdf)
- **Local scope is materially incomplete.** The coverage document reports 160 source-supported, model-reviewed printed-word labels out of 158,066 printed English words, including grammar and additions; it explicitly states that the requested semantic accuracy gate fails and that shared QF upstream data is not independent validation. Candidate lemma/gloss correspondences and rendered playback checks have different scopes. — [Existing coverage document](/home/sammy/.t3/worktrees/Beautiful-Quran/t3-d08c8ff8/docs/ENGLISH_ALIGNMENT_COVERAGE.md)

### Inferences

- QF word-card glosses can be valuable proposal evidence but cannot be treated as a complete gold map into a different prose translation. Joining them by Arabic position answers “what gloss is attached to this Arabic word?”, not “which exact printed Sahih words express that word or phrase?”.
- No surveyed resource justifies replacing the existing mapper with a ready-made authoritative table. A request to QF/QAC maintainers about unpublished or licensed prose-span annotations could resolve this uncertainty, but sending such a request requires user authorization.
- quran.ai agreement is evidence that a proposed label is grounded in its fetched material. It is not independent corroboration when both the app and MCP derive the relevant text/morphology from the same Quran.com/QF family of data.

### Gaps

- Whether QF, QAC, a publisher or an academic project privately maintains reviewed Sahih prose span alignments; the bounded public search cannot establish universal nonexistence.
- Exact rights to distribute alignment metadata derived using protected word cards. Public access, open-source tooling, or an upstream dataset’s blanket license do not by themselves settle underlying content rights.
- QAC syntactic treebank coverage sufficient for every long verse was not measured. The existence of a treebank should not be presented as a complete independent owner oracle.

## Which alignment methods are viable, and what do their published results actually establish?

### Takeaway

Contextual aligners and structured LLM phrase proposals are viable **offline proposal generators**. None of the verified papers establishes 99.9% Quran prose ownership; word-alignment F1/AER, model similarity scores, and structurally valid JSON are not the app’s all-printed-word exact-owner accuracy.

### Cited Findings

- **SimAlign:** uses pretrained static/contextual multilingual embeddings, with Argmax, Itermax and maximum-weight matching extraction. Itermax explicitly permits a token to link to multiple other tokens; maximum-weight matching at the word level is one-to-one. Subword links can be lifted to multiple word links. The paper also considers null links/untranslated words. Its original evaluation pairs English with Czech, German, Persian, French, Hindi and Romanian—**not Arabic**. One published setting, mBERT layer 8 with subword Argmax, has English–German F1 .81/AER .19 and English–Hindi F1 .55/AER .45 (Table 2). These are link-set metrics on those gold corpora, not ownership accuracy here. — [SimAlign paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.147.pdf)
- SimAlign’s official library takes pretokenized word lists and returns zero-based link pairs for multiple extraction methods. Available languages depend on the underlying multilingual model; a forward pass can run on CPU. Its dependency stack is Python/Transformers/Torch, not a small Android runtime component. mBERT’s documented supported-language list includes Arabic, which establishes applicability, not Arabic/Quran accuracy. — [SimAlign repository](https://github.com/cisnlp/simalign), [mBERT official multilingual documentation](https://github.com/google-research/bert/blob/master/multilingual.md)
- **awesome-align:** extracts mBERT links by thresholding forward/backward contextual probabilities and intersecting directions. It supports fine-tuning on parallel text, self-training and supervised gold links. Its original paper evaluates German, French, Romanian, Japanese and Chinese against English—**not Arabic**. In Table 2 its multilingual softmax setting with β=0 reports AER 15.3, 4.4, 22.6, 37.9 and 13.6 respectively. AER is based on predicted links versus sure/possible gold links, not the percentage of printed words assigned the correct app owner. — [awesome-align paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.181.pdf)
- awesome-align takes tokenized parallel pairs, outputs Pharaoh `i-j` links and optionally probabilities, supports supervised alignment training, and warns that increasing recall using consistency optimization can reduce precision. The probabilities are extractor outputs; the README does not establish calibration to a 99.9% semantic-confidence threshold. — [Official awesome-align repository](https://github.com/neulab/awesome-align)
- **Statistical methods:** fast_align is a reparameterized IBM Model 2 implementation. It trains from tokenized parallel sentence pairs, gives asymmetric forward/reverse links, and offers symmetrization via atools. eflomal offers lexical/HMM/fertility priors and low-memory estimation; null alignments are omitted from its link output. — [fast_align official repository](https://github.com/clab/fast_align), [eflomal official repository](https://github.com/robertostling/eflomal)
- **An Arabic-specific published comparison:** XL-WA’s English–Arabic test results (micro link precision/recall/F1, Table 2) are GIZA++ 66.0/57.8/61.6; FastAlign 68.6/66.3/67.4; SQuAD BERT 87.3/78.8/82.9; MultiMirror 88.3/77.9/82.8. Neural systems there were trained on silver labels produced using statistical systems. This quantifies one general-domain Arabic benchmark only; it does not predict Quran results. — [XL-WA paper, Table 2](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/102935e2-dea3-4598-9f63-fdb5cec8061a/Martelli_XL-WA_2023.pdf)
- **Supervised span/label alternatives:** WSPAlign pretrains with weak span labels from Wikipedia entity co-mentions and contextual embeddings, then offers benchmark-specific fine-tuned checkpoints. BinaryAlign reformulates alignment as binary sequence labeling, rather than forcing one contiguous answer span. Its official repository lists German/French/Romanian/Chinese/Japanese and Align6 checkpoints, and a noncommercial license. Those model/checkpoint choices require separate fit and licensing review before adoption. — [WSPAlign paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.621/), [WSPAlign repository](https://github.com/qiyuw/WSPAlign), [BinaryAlign paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12881), [BinaryAlign repository](https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-BinaryAlignWordAlignementasBinarySequenceLabeling)
- **Current encoder versus LLM evidence:** SemiAlign (ACL 2025) improves BinaryAlign via self-training and studies fine-tuned multilingual decoder LLMs. Main test languages are Japanese/German/Romanian/Chinese against English, with additional Swedish/Finnish/Hebrew settings—not Arabic or Quran. On KFTT Japanese–English, Table 1 reports BinaryAlign reproduced F1 86.07, Llama3.1-Instruct with supervised tuning 83.84, SemiAlign-E 87.62 and SemiAlign-D 88.36. Its training dependence matters: this is not evidence that a zero-shot frontier chat model will produce corpus-complete accurate alignments. — [SemiAlign paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1020.pdf)
- **Structured general LLM generation:** lexi-align accepts caller-supplied arbitrary tokens, including phrases, handles repeated token identities with unique markers, validates token existence and alignment lengths, and retries invalid output. Its preliminary official EN–SL XL-WA test result for GPT-4o-2024-08-06 with one example is precision .863, recall .829, F1 .846. That is English–Slovenian, with a particular historical model/prompt/library version; no Arabic/Quran accuracy follows. Its dynamic schema guarantees some structural properties, not correct meanings. — [lexi-align official repository](https://github.com/borh-lab/lexi-align)

### Inferences

Ranked fit for this finite corpus, rather than a ranking of universal NLP quality:

1. **Reviewed offline phrase map, with models proposing and humans adjudicating.** A finite, pinned translation can be aligned once. Evidence from QAC/glosses/tafsir plus independently checked phrase labels offers a tractable route to replacing runtime guesswork without adding a model to the client. A model can draft labels for all verses systematically; those labels remain proposals until reviewed under an explicit annotation policy.
2. **Contextual proposal ensemble plus existing lexical/QAC evidence.** SimAlign and awesome-align offer inexpensive contextual baselines relative to full LLM generation. Include Arabic→English and English→English word-card→prose proposals as different evidence channels. Require passage-specific semantic review wherever channels disagree, leave tokens null, or merge/split meanings. Shared mBERT ancestry means agreement between these two tools is not independent confirmation.
3. **Structured whole-verse LLM phrase proposals/review.** Most flexible for expansions, omitted subjects, contextual senses, bracketed explanations and grouped grammar. Prefer input-indexed spans to reproducing/retranslating prose. Cross-check against independent evidence and blind labels. Separate proposal and review prompts/models help find disagreements, but two agreeing LLMs do not establish a frequentist confidence guarantee. Opus can assess a concrete annotation policy/proposal; no Opus accuracy result was verified in this research.
4. **A small supervised aligner after a held-out gold pilot exists.** Binary sequence labels can represent disconnected links; single-span QA methods are attractive for phrase expansions but can miss discontinuous many-to-many links. Fine-tuning introduces training/evaluation machinery and dependencies. For a fixed 6,236-verse edition, directly storing reviewed labels may be thinner than building a bespoke predictive model.
5. **Statistical alignment as a cheap disagreement detector.** Gives a different model family, learned lexical/reordering/fertility signals and a reproducible baseline. The app’s 6,236 verse pairs are much smaller than many paper training corpora; rare terms, archaic Arabic, prose additions and repeated words make extrapolation uncertain. It should not be the final authority.

Feature-specific implications (inferred from the documented algorithms and app task):

| Requirement | What can help | What remains a policy/review problem |
|---|---|---|
| Reordering | Unrestricted contextual links, bidirectional statistical links, indexed LLM proposals | Semantic ownership must allow crossing links; the continuous wash is a separate deterministic reduction |
| One Arabic word → expanded English phrase | Itermax/subword links, span prediction, phrase-token LLM prompts | Ensure every modifier and grammatical word receives an explicit phrase policy, not just the obvious noun |
| Many Arabic words → one English meaning | Multiple links, fertility/sequence labeling, grouped phrase spans | Which onset triggers wash/tint/tap is not determined by a generic link set |
| Discontinuous/many-to-many realization | Binary sequence labeling, unrestricted link graphs | Avoid assuming every meaning can be represented by one contiguous single-owner span |
| Arabic meaning omitted in English | Explicit null/unaligned links | No printed token exists to highlight; absence must not become an invented owner |
| English grammar absent as a distinct Arabic word | Morphology and phrase attachment | Define acceptable anchors and adjudicate ties; literal token overlap cannot settle this |
| Editorial brackets/additions | Detect and retain tokens, source-supported phrase proposals | Brackets can carry interpretive additions or grammatical clarification; bracket status alone cannot justify excluding words from accuracy |
| Repeated homographs/polysemy | Contextual embeddings, vocalized lemmas, indexed occurrences | Dictionary/root equivalence and model similarity can still select the wrong occurrence or sense |

### Gaps

- No benchmarked result on this exact Sahih edition, its 158,066 tokens, its Arabic 77,429-word segmentation, or the app’s grammar/addition anchoring policy.
- No verified calibration mapping any aligner’s probability, LLM self-rated confidence or cross-model agreement to a semantic-error bound on this corpus.
- No verified recent Opus evaluation on Arabic–English prose alignment. A proposal/review by Opus is a review artifact, not a measured guarantee.
- Download/inference feasibility and compatibility of older Python repositories have not been tested. No weights or new dependencies were installed; eventual model use may require downloads exceeding the task’s 100 MB restriction and is a separate execution decision.

## How can systematic alignment stay offline-first and keep the runtime thin?

### Takeaway

Keep complex alignment and adjudication outside the clients; ship or synchronize compact, text-bound approved metadata and run only deterministic lookup plus the existing continuous-wash policy. Before implementation, settle what an “exact owner” means for many-to-many phrases and translator additions, then build a blind gold pilot that evaluates that definition on every printed token.

### Cited Findings

- The current `EnglishAlignment` already separates semantic `spans` from `washSpans`. It builds the wash by scanning semantic owners backward and taking a suffix minimum, so a later English meaning heard early carries the preceding English text into a continuous prefix. Its printed spans contain one Arabic position, not a set/range of semantic contributors. — [Current alignment types and wash policy](/home/sammy/.t3/worktrees/Beautiful-Quran/t3-d08c8ff8/app/src/main/java/com/beautifulquran/domain/EnglishWordAlignment.kt:8)
- The mapper’s own documentation describes lexical anchoring, guarded interpolation for unmatched text, approximate synonyms/ambiguous repetitions/additions, and once-per-verse runtime computation. Its implementation has separate rules for shared cards, grammar, morphology, sentence closure and bracketed additions. Thus a structurally complete output can still contain guessed owners. — [Current mapper](/home/sammy/.t3/worktrees/Beautiful-Quran/t3-d08c8ff8/app/src/main/java/com/beautifulquran/domain/EnglishWordAlignment.kt:35)
- The existing semantic gate validates exact printed token and character bounds, rejects duplicates/text changes, includes every printed word when accuracy is requested, and distinguishes candidate rules from reviewed reference labels. Timing queries and repeated rendered frames do not enlarge the semantic population. — [Coverage and certification contract](/home/sammy/.t3/worktrees/Beautiful-Quran/t3-d08c8ff8/docs/ENGLISH_ALIGNMENT_COVERAGE.md)
- The existing protected-content architecture keeps QF credentials only in Worker secrets, word/QCF content in separate synchronized device caches, and canonical public data in the packaged DB. Reader generations invalidate derived English/gloss views when upstream content changes; known canonical/QCF segmentation differences are explicit and fail closed. — [QF content architecture](/home/sammy/.t3/worktrees/Beautiful-Quran/t3-d08c8ff8/docs/QF_CONTENT_SYNC.md)

### Inferences

- **Minimal pilot before a rewrite:** freeze translation text, Arabic slot list and source revisions; define the annotation guide; build a stratified blind reference containing ordinary and difficult verses; run the existing mapper and one independent proposal method against it. A pilot determines whether a reviewed table, a small general phrase rule set, or training is worth the complexity. Published NLP scores cannot answer this architectural choice.
- **Separate semantic relation from presentation anchor.** Review metadata should allow an English span to relate to one Arabic word, several contributors, or no direct lexical counterpart; separately record the chosen app anchor and the rule/rationale for null/addition cases. Existing runtime APIs may consume one approved anchor per English token, preserving thinness while retaining richer evidence outside runtime. This is an annotation-policy proposal, not a claim that all meanings have a uniquely correct single Arabic owner.
- **Bind metadata to actual rendered text.** Include translation edition/revision/text hash, canonical Arabic slot hash, exact token/character bounds and mapping schema revision. Validate segmentation by the actual Arabic text, not matching counts or clamped positions. Reject a mismatched table instead of silently treating stale offsets as exact labels.
- **Derive wash from approved anchors; do not encode monotonicity into semantic labels.** If ownership is forced to be monotone to simplify ink, reordered meanings are lost for taps/repeat tint. Keep crossing semantic anchors and apply the present deterministic continuous-prefix reduction independently.
- **Preserve separate content/cache boundaries.** A table must not embed protected gloss text or bundled QF snapshots. If derived metadata is authorized for packaging, a small standalone public alignment asset can leave QF content in its present cache. If metadata must track protected revisions privately, deliver it through a revisioned compatible cache and purge/revoke appropriately. The legal/distribution choice remains unresolved; the architecture should not assume publication permission.
- **Do not proliferate client heuristics while researching.** Whole-corpus proposal generation plus an exhaustive disagreement/review queue changes the unit of work from individual user-reported failures to every printed token. The final client can become smaller even if the offline research process is richer. Automatic agreement may prioritize review but must not silently turn silver labels into gold.
- **Accuracy must follow the declared truth policy.** Evaluate all-word anchor accuracy separately from phrase-link precision/recall, unreviewed/null coverage, continuous ink, taps/repeats and playback transitions. Once ambiguity is explicitly represented, report correctness of the declared presentation anchor and uncertainty of the underlying semantic relation separately.

### Gaps

- Bilingual reviewer availability, annotation time/budget, independence and agreement rate have not been established.
- Whether the user accepts phrase anchors for non-uniquely-owned grammar/additions or requires another UI policy needs a concrete reviewed proposal, not a theoretical guarantee.
- Whether generated metadata can be committed, and which source revisions are distributable, needs a source-specific rights decision before implementation.
- Full-corpus independent semantic labels are absent. These notes do not establish 99.9% accuracy or confidence, and propose no production change until the pilot/policy review makes the tradeoff concrete.
